What We Handle

Cybersecurity fraud takes many forms. It can involve misrepresentation of security systems to government agencies, false billing for IT infrastructure that doesn't meet contract specs, inadequate data protection measures hidden from clients, or compliance violations in regulated industries like healthcare and finance. If your employer is submitting false claims tied to cybersecurity work—whether to Medicare, the Department of Defense, or other federal programs—you have both ethical and legal standing to act.

We prosecute these cases under the False Claims Act, a federal law that allows whistleblowers to sue on behalf of the government. You can also bring retaliation claims if your employer has fired you, demoted you, cut your pay, or harassed you for raising concerns internally or reporting to authorities.

Common Questions Whistleblowers Ask

What should I keep private if I'm considering reporting fraud? Don't discuss your concerns casually with coworkers, managers, or HR before you've spoken with a lawyer. Informal complaints can be minimized or mishandled. Avoid venting about stress or personal grievances to HR; focus only on the factual, illegal conduct when you do report. Never admit to minor policy violations yourself—HR's job is to protect the company, not you. Once you've documented the fraud and consulted with an attorney, a formal report is different; that's protected activity.

Is whistleblowing worth it financially? It depends on the evidence and the scale of the fraud. Strong documentation—emails, contracts, audit reports, communications showing knowledge of the false claims—makes your case much stronger. If the fraud is significant and affects government contracts or healthcare programs, the potential recovery justifies the fight. We've represented whistleblowers across Pennsylvania in cases involving hundreds of thousands of dollars in recovered compensation.

What happens if my employer retaliates? Retaliation is illegal. If you're fired, demoted, have your hours cut, or face harassment after reporting fraud, we can file a separate retaliation claim. These claims can proceed independently of the False Claims Act lawsuit and often resolve more quickly.
